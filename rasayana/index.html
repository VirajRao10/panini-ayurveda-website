Rasayana

Ayurveda's approach to rejuvenation and healthy ageing

A Dutch version of this article has also appeared in Ayurveda Magazine.

Rasayana refers to renewal and rejuvenation of the body's tissues and systems. It is a distinctive concept and specialised branch of Ayurveda.

In Ayurvedic tradition, treatments intended to nourish rasa and the other dhatus, support Ojas, strength, complexion, vitality, memory and longevity are described as Rasayana.

How did Rasayana come into existence?

The Charaka Samhita, an ancient Ayurvedic text, tells a story about the origins of Rasayana. It describes two groups of sages on the Indian subcontinent: the Shalin and the Yayavar. The Yayavar sages travelled from place to place to teach. According to the story, exposure to urban lifestyles and unfamiliar foods disturbed their health, leaving them too sluggish to perform their spiritual Sadhana.

At the foothills of the Himalayas, Indra, the compassionate king of the gods, is said to have received the sages and taught them rejuvenating Rasayana practices. Sages including Bhrigu, Angira, Atri, Kashyap and Agastya then learned, practised and shared this knowledge.

The story of sages seeking renewal after periods of unhealthy food and lifestyle has clear parallels with modern life.

What does the word Rasayana mean?

The word Rasayana combines rasa and ayana. Here, rasa refers to Rasa dhatu and, by extension, all seven dhatus; ayana or apyayana refers to nourishing, promoting and enhancing. Rasayana practices are therefore intended to nourish and rejuvenate the body's tissues.

Definition of Rasayana

Swasthasyorjaskaram yattu tadvrushyam tadrasayanam / Cha.Chi.1/1/5

Labhapayo hi shastanam rasadinam rasayanam / Cha. Chi. 1/1/8

The Charaka Samhita describes Rasayana as the use of herbs and treatments to invigorate a healthy person and promote the quality of Rasa dhatu.

Rasayanam hi tjdneyam yat jaravyadinashanam / Shrangadhar Samhita

The Sharangadhar Samhita describes Rasayana as that which helps keep disease and the effects of ageing at a distance.

Rasayanam naam vayahsthapanam ayurmedhabalakaram rogharansamartham cha / Su. Sa. 1

Traditional texts associate Rasayana with speech, healthy ageing, longevity, intelligence, memory, strength, complexion, vitality and resistance to disease.

Ayurveda teaches that nourishing rasa supports the other dhatus and, in turn, Ojas, vitality and resilience.

Important types of Rasayana

1. General rejuvenation and condition-specific support

Traditional examples include Shatavari or Ashwagandha powder taken with a suitable carrier, and Vardhaman Pippali Rasayana used under supervision following respiratory illness.

2. Rasayana for body and mind

Brahmi is traditionally regarded as a Rasayana for the nervous system, while good conduct, meditation, music, silence and mantra are described as Rasayana for the mind.

3. Kamya, Naimittika and Ajasrika Rasayana

Kamya: Kamya Rasayana is used with a specific goal (kamana) in mind. The duration depends on that goal and requires individual assessment.

Naimittika: Naimittika Rasayana is used for a limited period to support recovery after illness; Guduchi after fever or flu is a traditional example.

Ajasrika: Ajasrika Rasayana refers to ongoing daily support, such as a nourishing diet, regular exercise, constructive habits and appropriate use of foods such as ghee or milk.

4. Vatatapika and Kuti Praveshika Rasayana

Vatatapika Rasayana: This practical approach allows a person to continue normal daily life while following an appropriate diet, lifestyle and herbal programme. Traditional programmes may last from 21 days to one year.

Kuti Praveshika Rasayana: This intensive method takes place in a specially designed retreat or hut (kuti). For several weeks, the participant follows a structured routine involving a sattvic diet, meditation, silence and prescribed Rasayana preparations, followed by a gradual return to normal life.

Classical texts contain extraordinary accounts of renewal following Kuti Praveshika Rasayana. Because the method requires extensive preparation, resources and discipline, it is rarely practised today.

Who may be suited to Rasayana treatment?

  • Classical texts generally describe Rasayana for people up to about 70 years of age, subject to individual assessment.
  • A calm, sattvic mindset and the ability to follow the programme consistently are considered helpful.
  • Preparation may include appropriate cleansing under supervision.
  • Good Agni and minimal Ama are traditionally considered important before beginning.

When may Rasayana be unsuitable?

Classical texts caution against intensive Rasayana when a person cannot safely or consistently follow the required diet and lifestyle. Suitability should always be assessed individually, particularly where there are medical conditions, mental health concerns, substance dependence, pregnancy or prescribed medicines.

Anupana used with Rasayana

Anupana is a carrier chosen to complement or balance the properties of a Rasayana preparation. Milk, honey and ghee are commonly used; other traditional carriers include water, oil, sharkara and jaggery. The choice and combination require care and should be personalised.

Anupana may moderate a strong herb or direct a preparation towards a particular system. Traditional examples include milk with Pippali and ghee with Brahmi.

Preparing Rasayana

Some Rasayana preparations are simple, using the fresh juice or powder of a single herb with a specific anupana. Others combine many herbs and involve a complex preparation method, as in Chyawanprash.

Preparing Chyawanprash

Chyawanprash is a well-known Rasayana made primarily from Amalaki fruit together with many other ingredients. The Ashtanga Hridayam describes its preparation in detail.

A decoction is prepared from herbs including Ashtavarga and Dashamoola. Amalaki fruits are cooked in the decoction and made into a smooth pulp, which is then cooked with ghee and sesame oil. A syrup is prepared from the remaining decoction and sharkara. The Amalaki pulp is combined with the syrup, followed by ingredients such as cardamom, Pippali, Vanshlochan and honey.

Understanding the care and complexity involved in these preparations can deepen our appreciation of them.

Diet for Rasayana treatments

Freshly prepared, sattvic and easy-to-digest foods are traditionally recommended during Rasayana. Depending on the programme, this may include ghee, milk, honey, rice and other nourishing foods, with individual restrictions where needed.

Some classical programmes prescribe a very limited diet, while particular preparations have their own food restrictions. These regimens should not be attempted without qualified supervision.

The Charaka Samhita also describes a positive mind, ethical conduct and a joyful sattvic lifestyle as forms of Manas or Achara Rasayana. The Ashtanga Hridayam similarly emphasises constructive behaviour, positivity and a calm mind.

Can you design your own Rasayana treatment?

Herbal Rasayana requires consideration of the tissues and systems involved, the person's Prakriti, the season, the choice of herbs and anupana, the level of Ama and readiness for treatment.

Diet, fasting, cleansing and Panchakarma may also need to be considered. For these reasons, consult a qualified Ayurvedic professional before beginning a Rasayana programme.

Personal guidance

Considering Rasayana support?

Rasayana choices depend on constitution, digestion, season, health history and readiness for herbs or cleansing. A consultation can help you choose a suitable path.
