Skin Diseases and Ayurvedic Treatments

By Senior Ayurvedic Consultant Alaknanda Rao (B.A.M.S.)

The skin reflects a person's health. Everyone wants soft, supple, clear skin with a healthy glow.

The skin is considered a seat of Vata dosha. Kapha helps maintain its softness, while Pitta gives the skin a healthy glow. The skin is responsible for the senses of touch and temperature, thermal regulation, the removal of liquid waste through sweat and the protection of the body's organs.

Healthy, soft and supple skin needs attention from the inside out. Skin care begins with drinking enough water. Eating a healthy diet, exercising regularly, avoiding very spicy, heavy, fried, oily and acidic foods, limiting exposure to intense sunlight and avoiding harsh chemicals are basic principles for preventing skin problems.

Ayurvedic guidance can help you choose foods and a skincare routine suited to your constitution and skin type.

Although everyone wants healthy skin, skin problems are very common. Redness, dryness, itching and burning can make daily life difficult. Even painless skin conditions may affect emotional wellbeing because of the embarrassment they can cause. For example, acne or vitiligo may contribute to low self-esteem in teenagers.

Dryness can be the beginning of many skin problems. The simple technique of Abhyanga, or self-massage, can help prevent dry skin and improve circulation. Applying warm sesame oil to the body before showering helps keep the skin soft, nourished and healthy.

Different concerns—including oily skin, acne, rashes, psoriasis, eczema, abscesses, infections, herpes, allergies, hives, rosacea, fungal nail infections and lichen sclerosus—require different approaches.

From an Ayurvedic perspective, an assessment is based on the three Doshas: Vata, Pitta and Kapha. The depth and duration of the problem help determine the plan and choice of herbs. Identifying and avoiding factors that contribute to the skin condition is an important part of treatment. Dietary adjustments, lifestyle changes, internal purification, deep skin cleansing and herbal formulations may all play a role.

Internal and external approaches work together. Panchakarma cleansing treatments can be useful for some chronic conditions. After symptoms improve, supportive remedies may be used to strengthen the skin and reduce recurrence.

Tips for beautiful skin

For the face

  • If coconut oil suits your skin, it can be used for facial massage or to remove make-up. Patch-test it first.
  • Use a gentle cleanser suited to your skin; aloe vera gel may be an option for some people.
  • Rose water or cucumber juice may be used as a light toner if your skin tolerates them.

For the body

  • Abhyanga (self-massage) with sesame oil.
  • Body scrub: mix two teaspoons of red lentil powder or chickpea flour with water to make a paste. Use it in the shower as a natural cleanser instead of soap. It removes excess oil while leaving the skin nourished.
  • Turmeric is traditionally used in Ayurveda, but it can interact with medicines and is not suitable for everyone. Seek personalised advice before using it regularly.
