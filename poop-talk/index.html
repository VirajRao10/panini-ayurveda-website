Poop Talk

Constipation and bowel health through an Ayurvedic lens

For many of us, talking about bowel movements is taboo and can feel uncomfortable. Ayurveda, however, gives this topic great importance. Your Ayurvedic consultant will therefore discuss bowel habits with you regularly.

Why?

A regular bowel movement can make a comfortable start to the day. Afterwards, you may feel energetic, clear-headed and ready for breakfast.

Constipation may be accompanied by gas, bloating, acidity, nausea, reduced appetite, a feeling of heaviness, headaches, haemorrhoids or anal fissures.

What is normal?

A waste product can provide information about the process that produced it. Similarly, stools can offer clues about digestion and absorption. When the digestive fire works well, food is digested fully and bowel movements are regular. The body can then absorb nutrients effectively and turn them into energy.

Normally, a person can go to the toilet first thing in the morning without stimulants such as coffee or tea. A regular bowel movement once or twice a day can be normal.

Like a ripe banana?

A healthy stool is often described as being shaped like a ripe banana: formed, but neither too hard nor too soft. It should pass without excessive straining and leave you feeling comfortably emptied.

What is not normal?

Irregular bowel movements, repeatedly skipping days, hard pellets, very loose stools, frequent urgency or a persistent feeling of incomplete emptying may indicate that your digestion needs attention.

Undigested food, mucus, blood or suspected parasites in the stool should be discussed with an appropriate healthcare professional.

Other signs that merit attention include cramping, bloating, gas, heaviness or sleepiness after meals, burning, belching, nausea, intestinal rumbling, diarrhoea, abdominal discomfort, anal itching or pain, haemorrhoids and fissures.

What can help?

Regular mealtimes, a diet suited to your constitution and the season, adequate rest, appropriate exercise and sufficient sleep can all support regular bowel movements.

Balanced meals containing a modest amount of oil or ghee may be helpful. In Ayurveda, people with constipation are often advised to favour warm, cooked, soft and moist foods. Spices such as ginger, fennel, cumin, black pepper, curry leaves, asafoetida and ajwain are traditionally used to support digestion.

Drinking enough water is also important; some people find warm water more comfortable.

The liver plays an important role in digestion. Persistent constipation should be assessed properly rather than treated with unsupervised cleansing.

Ayurvedic approaches may include dietary changes, fasting or mono-diets, herbs, Panchakarma, purgation or enemas. These approaches are not suitable for everyone and should only be undertaken with qualified guidance.

Stress can also affect digestion and bowel habits. Creating time to relax may help support a more comfortable routine.

Tips for constipation

  • Try to maintain a regular toilet routine, ideally allowing enough time before breakfast.
  • Raising your feet on a small stool while using a modern toilet can create a more comfortable squatting position.
  • Gentle yoga postures and breathing practices may support relaxation and digestion when they are appropriate for you.
  • Soaked or cooked raisins, prunes, apricots or figs may help some people.
  • Some Ayurvedic routines use a teaspoon of ghee or olive oil in warm water or milk at bedtime; seek personalised advice if you are unsure whether this is suitable for you.

Ayurvedic practice uses a range of preparations for constipation, but their effects and safety differ. Do not use strong laxative herbs, castor oil, purgation or enemas without appropriate professional advice.

Every case of constipation is different, and self-medication can make matters worse. A proper assessment can help identify the cause. Personalised dietary and herbal advice from an experienced Ayurvedic consultant can support bowel health and help relieve constipation.
